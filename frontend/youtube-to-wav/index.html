🎚️ Why Music Producers & Editors Choose WAV Over MP3

When sampling audio from YouTube or pulling background scores for video editing, downloading as MP3 causes cumulative generation loss. Because YouTube already compresses audio into Opus or AAC upon upload, re-encoding it into MP3 compresses the file a second time—permanently smearing high frequencies and introducing metallic digital artifacts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is YouTube audio really uncompressed when converted to WAV?
YouTube streams audio in compressed formats (Opus up to 160kbps or AAC up to 256kbps). Converting to WAV does not recreate frequencies that were never uploaded, but it prevents secondary generation loss. When you convert to MP3, the file is compressed twice. Converting directly to WAV keeps 100% of the YouTube stream's original clarity with zero additional loss.

What is the difference between WAV and FLAC?
Both are lossless audio formats. WAV is uncompressed PCM audio, making it the universal standard for music production and video editing timelines. FLAC is compressed lossless audio, meaning it retains the exact same pristine sound quality as WAV but has approximately 50% smaller file size.
Why is WAV better than MP3 for music production and video editing?
MP3 cuts out subtle audio frequencies to save disk space. When you pitch-down, time-stretch, or heavily EQ an MP3 in a DAW like FL Studio, harsh digital artifacts appear. Uncompressed WAV audio handles time-stretching, pitching, and video timeline sync cleanly with zero digital distortion.
