How to Clip & Download a YouTube Live Stream in 2026 (Longer Than 60 Seconds)

Published by YTCuttr Team • Updated November 26, 2026 • 7 min read
Quick Answer Since YouTube retired viewer-made Clips in April 2026, viewers can only share a Start at Timestamp link natively. To download an actual MP4 highlight (up to 5 minutes long in 1080p or 4K) from a recorded 4-to-10-hour live stream without downloading the whole VOD, paste the stream URL into YTCuttr.com and slice just your timestamps.

YouTube live streams—whether gaming marathons, esports tournaments, live podcasts, or webinars—frequently run three to eight hours long. When an unforgettable two-minute clutch play or interview exchange happens at hour four, downloading an entire 15GB VOD file just to grab one scene is painfully slow.

Even worse, legacy browser clippers like YTCutter and ClipsCutter frequently time out or cap exports at 360p when fed multi-hour stream URLs, and YouTube’s own native viewer clipping scissors icon was retired in April 2026. Here is how to clip YouTube live streams cleanly today depending on whether you are the channel owner or an editor/viewer.

1. How to Extract a Clip from Any Archived YouTube Live Stream (1080p / 4K MP4)

Once a public YouTube live stream ends and finishes processing into a VOD, you can slice out segments up to 5 minutes long without downloading the rest of the broadcast:

  1. Find the Exact Highlight Timestamp: Play the archived live stream on YouTube (or search the stream’s auto-transcript via Show transcript in the description to jump straight to a spoken phrase). Note the exact start and end time (e.g., 03:42:10 to 03:44:25).
  2. Paste the Stream URL into YTCuttr: Open ytcuttr.com (or the 4K Cutter if the broadcast streamed in 1440p/2160p) and click Get Video.
  3. Lock In Your Start & End Window: Enter your HH:MM:SS timestamps or use the preview player controls. Keep each individual cut under 5 minutes (300 seconds).
  4. Download Clean MP4: Choose 1080p60, 1080p, or 720p and click Download Clip. YTCuttr pulls only that timestamp range on the server, avoiding multi-hour downloads and 360p paywalls.

2. How Streamers Clip Their Own Broadcasts in YouTube Studio

If you own the YouTube channel that hosted the live stream, YouTube automatically archives broadcasts under 12 hours long:

  • Download Full Archive: Open YouTube Studio → Content → Live, hover over the broadcast, open the three-dot menu, and click Download (note: YouTube exports creator archives at 720p max in many cases, whereas stream-slicing retains 1080p60/4K).
  • Create Shorts / Clips Inside Studio: Inside YouTube Studio’s Video Clips / Remix tools, creators can still turn segments of their own streams into Shorts directly on their channel.

4. Tips for Multi-Hour Gaming & Podcast VODs

  • Wait 10–15 Minutes After Stream End: Very fresh live streams take a few minutes for YouTube to finalize high-bitrate 1080p60/4K DASH streams. If you only see 360p/720p immediately after a stream ends, wait briefly and re-fetch.
  • Use 0.3s Pre-Roll for Gaming Reactions: Start your cut half a second before the kill/callout so the viewer gets full audio context before the action peaks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I download a clip from a YouTube live stream without downloading the whole 8-hour VOD?

Yes. Paste the archived live stream URL into YTCuttr, enter your exact start and end timestamps, and only that segment (up to 5 minutes per request) is processed and saved as an MP4.

Why can I no longer use YouTube’s Clip button on live streams?

YouTube retired viewer-created Clips in April 2026 in favor of Share at Timestamp links. See our YouTube Clip Button Missing guide for details.

How do I download my own past YouTube live stream?

If your stream was under 12 hours, go to YouTube Studio → Content → Live tab, click the options menu on your broadcast, and choose Download.

What if the live stream is still broadcasting right now?

URL timestamp cutters require a finished, archived VOD with fixed timestamps. Wait until the broadcast ends, or use OBS Studio’s Replay Buffer locally if you are the broadcaster.
