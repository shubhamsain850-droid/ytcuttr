Why Resolution Matters When Converting 16:9 Video to 9:16 Shorts

Standard horizontal YouTube videos have a 16:9 aspect ratio (1920×1080 or 3840×2160). Vertical Shorts and Reels use 9:16 (1080×1920). When you center-crop a horizontal frame to vertical, you keep only about 31% of the original width:

  • If you start with a 360p or 720p free clip (the ceiling on ClipsCutter and Kapwing’s free plans), cropping vertically leaves you with blurry sub-480p footage.
  • If you extract the segment in 1080p or 4K using YTCuttr’s 4K Video Cutter, the cropped vertical frame stays crisp on modern OLED phone screens.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why should I extract clips in 1080p or 4K before making a vertical 9:16 Short?

Cropping a horizontal 16:9 frame into vertical 9:16 discards two-thirds of the horizontal pixels. Starting from 1080p or 4K keeps faces and subtitles razor-sharp.

Can YouTube Studio convert my own video into a Short automatically?

Yes—on your own channel, YouTube’s Edit into a Short remix option lets you grab up to 60 seconds. Extracting the clean MP4 gives you full multi-cam framing, B-roll, and custom caption styling.
